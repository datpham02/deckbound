Terms used below, in the plain words the cards use (pillar 3: no glossary needed to read a card; this list is for implementers):

- **Deck** means every page the Scrivener owns in this run that is not bound: draw pile + hand + discard. The deck floor counts the deck, not the draw pile.
- **Draw** takes the top page of the draw pile into the hand. If the draw pile is empty, the discard is shuffled into the draw pile first (**reshuffle**). If both are empty, nothing is drawn (E15).
- **Deal N damage** to an enemy: subtract from the enemy's Guard first, then its health.
- **Gain N Guard**: adds to the Scrivener's Guard.
- **Heal N**: raises health, never above `hero_health`.
- **Lose N health**: subtracts from health directly; Guard does not stop it.
- **Enemy attacks you**: an enemy action with the attack telegraph resolves against the Scrivener, whether or not Guard absorbed all of it.
- **Water-stain**: the foul page (§5 page 21) the deck-fouler enemy adds to the discard.
- **Remove from the run**: the page is deleted from deck, hand, discard and slots for the rest of the run (the same removal a sever performs).
- **Binding**: a page in a bind slot. **Intact** bindings apply their Bind text; **frayed** ones apply nothing until end of combat.

## 3. Page writing rules

Every page in M1 obeys all of these. They exist so that the compact face stays readable on a phone (risk 3), every effect has an on-screen representation that T11–T13 already build (pillar 3), and the rules engine stays small (T6).

1. **Name** at most 20 characters. **Compact Play line** and **compact Bind line** at most 24 characters each, written here, never generated by truncating the full text (E9, risk 3). Numbers in compact lines are the same numbers as in the full text.
2. **Ink cost** is 0, 1, 2 or 3 and is the same for Play and Bind (concept: "spends the same Ink cost").
3. **No mid-play choice except a target.** A Play face either needs one enemy target (drag onto an enemy) or none (drag onto the play area). No page asks the player to pick a page from a list, order the deck, or choose between modes. Reward and rest screens are the only choice lists in M1.
4. **No timed statuses on the Scrivener.** The Scrivener's visible state is health, Guard and the three bind slots (E11, E13). No page grants an effect that lasts "until your next turn" or "for 2 turns", because that would need a status display T13 does not have. Bindings are the only lasting effects.
5. **Bind faces are passives; Play faces are instants.** A Bind text is one of: a start-of-combat effect, a start-of-turn effect, an end-of-turn effect, an end-of-combat effect, a trigger ("whenever ..."), or a standing modifier ("... deal 1 more"). A Play text resolves completely when played.
6. **Every Bind face can be frayed and severed** (pillar 2). No page says "cannot be frayed" or "cannot be severed". Pages may redirect a fray (Wax Seal) or react to one (Candle Debt, Knotted Thread), never ignore one.
7. **A binding never triggers on its own fraying.** A fray turns the page off before "whenever a binding is frayed" is evaluated, so Candle Debt and Knotted Thread fire for *other* bindings only, which is what their text says.
8. **Passive order** is slot order, slot 1 first. Standing modifiers stack additively.
9. **Nib Cut's "deal 1 more"** applies to every "deal N damage" in a *played* page, once per enemy hit; it does not apply to damage from bindings (Pressed Thorn, Lantern Oath, Drowned Codicil, Sealed Lien, Blotting Sheet bind).
10. **Draw and Ink passives obey §7.**
11. **Originality** (E7): every name, every line of text and every mechanic combination is written for the Chancery. Mechanics in general use in the genre (a basic strike, a basic guard, draw, energy, thorns, start-of-combat guard, a junk card) are allowed; a name, a text line or a signature combination from Slay the Spire, Slay the Spire 2, Monster Train, Inscryption or Griftlands is not. §8 records the check per page.

## 4. Rule interactions T6 and T7 must implement

- **Start of combat** order: start-of-combat passives in slot order (Inkwell Ward Guard, Drowned Codicil damage), then the first draw (including draw passives), then enemy telegraphs are shown.
- **Start of player turn** order: Guard is set to 0, then start-of-turn passives in slot order (Ink, Guard, health loss), then the base draw of `hand_draw`, then draw passives, then Footnote's kept page is already in hand (it never left).
- **End of player turn** order: end-of-turn passives in slot order (Lantern Oath damage), then Footnote keeps one page, then the rest of the hand is discarded, then enemies act.
- **Reshuffle** fires Ledger of Debts whenever the discard is shuffled into the draw pile, at most once per draw request.
- **Water-stain added** fires Blotting Sheet each time a stain enters the discard (one foul action that adds two stains fires twice).
- **Empty bind slot** means a slot with no page in it. A frayed slot is not empty. A severed slot is empty.
- **Enemy Guard** exists (T5 gives at least one enemy a guard action). Docket Stamp's bind reduces every enemy Guard gain by 3, to a minimum of 0.
- **Deck floor and removal.** The floor refuses binds only (E15). Pages that remove Water-stains from the run (Water-stain's own Play, Errata Slip, Blotting Sheet) ignore the floor, because they only ever remove pages an enemy added. No M1 page removes any other page from the run.
- **Water-stain in slots.** A bound Water-stain follows E2 and E5 exactly like any page: it leaves the deck, fills a slot, applies nothing, and comes back to the deck when unbound at a rest.

### 1. Nib Cut — 1 Ink

- **Play:** Deal 6 damage to an enemy.
- **Bind:** Pages you play that deal damage deal 1 more.
- **When you play it:** every turn. It is the deck's basic strike, four copies in the starting deck, and the first Ink of most turns goes here.
- **When you bind it:** after two or three rewards, when the deck has six or more damage pages and a fourth Nib Cut is the weakest of them. Binding one removes a starter and gives every Lantern Oath, Blank Folio and Candle Debt a little more.
- **When you regret it:** you bound it in a deck that drifted to Guard and draw, so a slot is spent on "deal 1 more" to two pages; or the frayer turns it off in the long warden fight you bound it for. Playing it is never regretted, which is why it is the starter.

### 2. Inkwell Ward — 1 Ink

- **Play:** Gain 5 Guard.
- **Bind:** Start each combat with 5 Guard.
- **When you play it:** an attack is telegraphed and the hand has Ink left after striking. Three copies in the starting deck make it the default answer to the plain attacker.
- **When you bind it:** the map ahead is attackers and the warden, and the opening enemy turn keeps costing health. Binding a copy also thins a starter out of the deck.
- **When you regret it:** you bound it before meeting the frayer, whose first telegraph is a fray, so the next fight opens with no Guard and a dead slot; or you played it on a turn the enemy was guarding, not attacking, and the Guard vanished at your next turn.

### 3. Torn Margin — 1 Ink

- **Play:** Draw 2 pages.
- **Bind:** Draw 1 more page at the start of each turn.
- **When you play it:** the hand is Ink-light (two Guard pages and a Footnote) and the deck has strikes left in it.
- **When you bind it:** the deck is eight pages or fewer and cycles every two turns; one more draw means seeing the whole deck most turns. It is the first draw passive and is capped by §7.
- **When you regret it:** the deck is fifteen pages with three Water-stains in it, so the extra draw is a stain as often as not; or the frayer takes it in the fight you planned around a six-page hand.

### 4. Pressed Thorn — 1 Ink

- **Play:** Deal 4 damage to an enemy.
- **Bind:** Whenever an enemy attacks you, deal 3 damage to it.
- **When you play it:** a cheap finisher for an enemy on 4 or less, or spare Ink at the end of a turn.
- **When you bind it:** fights with two or three attackers, where it returns 6 to 9 damage per enemy turn for nothing.
- **When you regret it:** bound before a stretch of frayers and foulers, which attack rarely, so the slot sat idle; or the boss severs it and the deck has no 4-damage page left for finishing.

### 5. Tally Mark — 1 Ink

- **Play:** Gain 2 Ink.
- **Bind:** Gain 1 more Ink at the start of each turn.
- **When you play it:** any turn with a 2-cost page you cannot otherwise afford; it costs 1 and gives 2, so it also thins the turn's hand.
- **When you bind it:** early, in most runs: 4 Ink a turn turns a 2-page turn into a 3-page turn for the whole floor. It is the "bound too early" page the vision statement talks about.
- **When you regret it:** the frayer turns it off and the hand is three 2-cost pages on 3 Ink; or the warden's fray telegraph points at it and the only Guard page is in the discard. Playing it the turn before you could have bound it is the small regret.

### 6. Candle Debt — 2 Ink

- **Play:** Deal 14 damage to an enemy. Add a Water-stain to your discard.
- **Bind:** Whenever another binding of yours is frayed, gain 2 Ink at the start of your next turn.
- **When you play it:** the enemy dies now and the stain is a problem for later; against the warden it is the best 2 Ink in the deck.
- **When you bind it:** the map ahead is fray-heavy (warden, frayers) and you wear Wax Seal, so every fray that lands pays 2 Ink the next turn.
- **When you regret it:** played three times in one fight, it leaves three Water-stains in a ten-page deck; bound on a floor with one frayer, it is a dead slot that cost 2 Ink; frayed itself, it pays nothing.

### 7. Wax Seal — 2 Ink

- **Play:** Gain 8 Guard.
- **Bind:** When an enemy would fray another binding of yours, this binding is frayed instead.
- **When you play it:** a large attack is telegraphed, or the boss's sever is one turn away and health matters more than the slot.
- **When you bind it:** you have one binding you cannot afford to lose (Tally Mark, Torn Margin) and a fray is coming. Once Wax Seal is frayed it applies nothing until end of combat, so it takes exactly one fray per fight.
- **When you regret it:** the boss severs instead of fraying, and Wax Seal does nothing against a sever; or it cost 2 Ink and a slot to protect a binding the enemies never targeted.

### 8. Lantern Oath — 2 Ink

- **Play:** Deal 5 damage to each enemy.
- **Bind:** At the end of each of your turns, deal 2 damage to each enemy.
- **When you play it:** three enemies on the right; 15 damage for 2 Ink.
- **When you bind it:** a floor of two- and three-enemy fights, where 4 to 6 damage every turn for no Ink adds up.
- **When you regret it:** the warden and the boss are alone, and 2 damage a turn is a slow slot against 50 or 90 health; or you played it into a single enemy for 5.

### 9. Errata Slip — 1 Ink

- **Play:** Remove every Water-stain in your hand and discard from the run.
- **Bind:** After each combat you win, remove every Water-stain in your deck from the run.
- **When you play it:** the fouler has added two or three stains and the hand is clogged; 1 Ink clears them all.
- **When you bind it:** the map has more than one fouler ahead. Stains still hurt during a fight, but never carry between fights. The passive fires at the end of the fight, so a fray during the fight stops it (pillar 2).
- **When you regret it:** no fouler on the chosen path, so both faces do nothing; or the frayer turns it off in the fouler fight, the stains stay, and the slot could have held Pressed Thorn.

### 10. Footnote — 0 Ink

- **Play:** Draw a page.
- **Bind:** At the end of your turn, your highest-cost page stays in your hand instead of being discarded (leftmost if tied).
- **When you play it:** always worth playing for free unless the draw pile is empty and the discard is empty; it replaces itself.
- **When you bind it:** the deck has Drowned Codicil or Oath of the Dry Hand, so a 3-cost page drawn with 1 Ink left is held for the next turn instead of lost. Binding costs 0 Ink, so it is also the cheapest way to thin a page.
- **When you regret it:** the highest-cost page in hand is a Water-stain and it keeps coming back; or a slot holding a 0-cost page is a slot not holding Tally Mark.

### 11. Docket Stamp — 2 Ink

- **Play:** Remove all Guard from an enemy, then deal 6 damage to it.
- **Bind:** Enemies gain 3 less Guard.
- **When you play it:** the enemy's telegraph is guard and it already has 8 Guard; Nib Cut would hit for nothing.
- **When you bind it:** the floor has guarding enemies on most nodes (T5 gives the plain attacker a guard action), so every strike lands fuller.
- **When you regret it:** fights of frayers and foulers that never guard; or played on an enemy with no Guard for 6 damage at 2 Ink, when Nib Cut did 6 for 1.

### 12. Sealed Lien — 2 Ink

- **Play:** Deal 7 damage to an enemy and gain 4 Guard.
- **Bind:** Whenever you gain Guard, deal 1 damage to each enemy.
- **When you play it:** a turn that needs both damage and Guard, which is most turns against two attackers.
- **When you bind it:** the deck leans on Guard (three Inkwell Wards, Wax Seal, Knotted Thread), so each Guard page also chips every enemy.
- **When you regret it:** a damage-and-draw deck where the only Guard gain is Inkwell Ward's start of combat; or the slot was needed for Lantern Oath, which does the same job better in three-enemy fights.

### 13. Drowned Codicil — 3 Ink

- **Play:** Deal 20 damage to an enemy.
- **Bind:** At the start of each combat, deal 6 damage to each enemy.
- **When you play it:** the warden or the boss, where 20 damage for the whole turn's Ink is still the fastest clock in the deck.
- **When you bind it:** a long floor of common fights with 20-health enemies, where 6 to every enemy before the first turn shortens every fight by a turn.
- **When you regret it:** binding costs 3 Ink, a whole turn of doing nothing else; and the boss severs it in the fight where you wanted the 20. Held in hand with 2 Ink, it is a dead draw unless Footnote is bound.

### 14. Salt Line — 1 Ink

- **Play:** Heal 4.
- **Bind:** After each combat you win, heal 3.
- **When you play it:** health is low mid-fight and the enemy's telegraph is not an attack, so the Ink is free.
- **When you bind it:** early on the floor with five or more fights ahead. Rests in M1 unbind, they do not heal, so this is the only steady healing.
- **When you regret it:** bound with two fights left before the boss, 6 health for a slot; or played at full health because nothing else was in hand.

### 15. Knotted Thread — 1 Ink

- **Play:** Gain 3 Guard for each binding you have.
- **Bind:** Whenever another binding of yours is frayed, gain 6 Guard.
- **When you play it:** two or three bindings worn, so 1 Ink buys 6 to 9 Guard.
- **When you bind it:** the warden is next and you wear bindings you expect to lose; each fray that lands pays Guard against the attack that comes with it.
- **When you regret it:** played with no bindings for 0 Guard; bound as your only binding, so there is nothing else to be frayed and it never triggers.

### 16. Blank Folio — 1 Ink

- **Play:** Deal 3 damage to an enemy for each empty bind slot.
- **Bind:** At the start of each turn, gain 2 Guard for each empty bind slot.
- **When you play it:** early, with three empty slots: 9 damage for 1 Ink, the best rate in the deck.
- **When you bind it:** a cautious run with one other binding; it fills a slot and the two empty ones left give 4 Guard a turn for nothing.
- **When you regret it:** every binding you add shrinks both faces; with three bindings worn it is a 1-Ink page that does nothing. A sever, oddly, makes it stronger, which is the only consolation a sever offers.

### 17. Ledger of Debts — 2 Ink

- **Play:** Deal 1 damage to an enemy for each page in your discard.
- **Bind:** Whenever your discard is shuffled into your deck, gain 5 Guard.
- **When you play it:** a fat deck late in a fight, when the discard is 12 pages and the enemy has 12 health.
- **When you bind it:** a thin deck that reshuffles every two turns, so the Guard arrives every other turn.
- **When you regret it:** played on turn 1 with an empty discard for 0; bound in a fifteen-page deck that reshuffles once a fight.

### 18. Blotting Sheet — 1 Ink

- **Play:** Deal 7 damage to an enemy. Remove a Water-stain from your hand from the run.
- **Bind:** Whenever a Water-stain is added to your discard, deal 4 damage to each enemy.
- **When you play it:** always a fair strike at 1 Ink; against the fouler it also cleans a stain out of the hand.
- **When you bind it:** the path has foulers, so every foul action costs the enemies 4 each and the fouler's own plan hurts it.
- **When you regret it:** no fouler ahead, so the bind face never fires and the 7-damage play is gone from the deck.

### 19. Oath of the Dry Hand — 3 Ink

- **Play:** Gain 15 Guard.
- **Bind:** Enemy attacks deal 1 less damage to you.
- **When you play it:** the boss's biggest attack is telegraphed and the whole turn can go to surviving it.
- **When you bind it:** fights of many small attacks (three attackers, two hits each) where 1 less per hit is 6 health a turn.
- **When you regret it:** 3 Ink to bind is a turn lost; against one enemy that hits once for 12, the bind saves 1. Played when the telegraph was a fray, the 15 Guard did nothing for the binding.

### 20. Flooded Index — 0 Ink

- **Play:** Draw a page. Lose 2 health.
- **Bind:** At the start of each turn, draw 1 more page and lose 1 health.
- **When you play it:** digging for the strike that ends the fight this turn, when 2 health is cheaper than an enemy turn.
- **When you bind it:** a thin, fast deck with Salt Line bound to pay the health back; the second draw passive, capped by §7.
- **When you regret it:** a long boss fight bleeds 1 a turn for twelve turns; a stain-filled deck draws stains. When the frayer turns it off, the bleeding stops with the drawing, which is the only fray a player may be glad of.

### 21. Water-stain — 1 Ink (foul page)

- **Play:** Remove this page from the run.
- **Bind:** No effect. The page fills the slot until you unbind it at a rest.
- **When you play it:** 1 Ink to spare and the stain would otherwise return every reshuffle.
- **When you bind it:** a spare slot and no Ink to spare in a fouler fight: binding takes it out of the deck for the run, and a rest can put it back and free the slot.
- **When you regret it:** the 1 Ink was the Nib Cut that would have ended the fight; the slot it fills is the one the next reward wanted.

Water-stain is added by the deck-fouler's foul action (T5) and by Candle Debt's Play. It is never offered as a reward. It is a page like any other for E1, E2, E5 and E9: it shows both faces, both can be used, it fills a slot when bound, and it returns when unbound.

## 6. Starting deck and reward pool

**Starting deck** (10 pages, `data/run/rules.json` `starting_deck` by id, T4b writes it):

| Page | Copies |
|---|---|
| Nib Cut | 4 |
| Inkwell Ward | 3 |
| Torn Margin | 1 |
| Pressed Thorn | 1 |
| Tally Mark | 1 |

Why these ten: the first hand of five teaches strike, Guard, draw, Ink and the first binding worth wearing (Pressed Thorn or Tally Mark) within the first fight. With 3 Ink and `hand_draw` 5, turn 1 is two or three plays. With 3 slots the starting deck can never reach the floor of 5 (10 − 3 = 7), so the floor only matters once stains are removed or T19 raises slots or lowers the floor; §10 criterion 9 says how QA still tests E15.

**Reward pool** (T15 offers three distinct pages from it, equal weight, or none): the 18 pages marked "yes" in the §5 table. Nib Cut and Inkwell Ward are starters only; Water-stain is never offered. Duplicates of pages already owned may be offered.

**Rest:** unbind one page or skip (M1 scope); no page in this set changes rests.

## 7. Draw and Ink passive caps (M1 risk 2)

These are the rules that stop a thin deck from looping. They are rules in `data/run/rules.json` and tests in T7, not page text.

| # | Cap | Start value | Why it holds |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | A bound page's passive draws at most 1 page per player turn. | 1 per slot | Torn Margin and Flooded Index are the only draw passives; each is +1. |
| D2 | No binding draws in response to playing, discarding or drawing a page. Only Play faces draw mid-turn. | rule | Removes every "draw on draw" loop; mid-turn draws are bounded by Ink. |
| D3 | Hand cap `hand_max`: a draw that would exceed it is not made. | 8 | `hand_draw` 5 + three draw slots would be 8 at most, and only two draw passives exist. |
| D4 | A bound page's passive gives at most 1 Ink per turn. | 1 per slot | Tally Mark is the only Ink passive; Candle Debt's 2 Ink needs a fray by an enemy and cannot be caused by the player. |
| D5 | Deck floor `deck_floor` refuses a bind (E15). | 5 | Draw passives cannot see more than the deck; with 5 pages and 7 draws, the reshuffle happens once and then draws nothing. |
| D6 | Empty draw pile and empty discard draw nothing (E15). | rule | Ends every turn in finite draws. |

Worst case with all caps: slots hold Torn Margin, Flooded Index and Tally Mark, deck of 5 pages. Each turn: 7 draw requests, 5 pages seen, 4 Ink, at most 4 plays. Each Play draw (Torn Margin, Footnote, Flooded Index) is paid in Ink or health, so a turn is at most about ten actions and always ends.

## 11. Open points for the reviewer

- **Fray and Guard.** This note assumes fray and sever land regardless of Guard (§2). T5 may instead let Guard block the attack that carries a fray; no page here depends on either reading, but Wax Seal and Knotted Thread get weaker if Guard already defends bindings. Decide in T5; this note does not change.
- **E15 on device.** With `bind_slots` 3 and a 10-page start deck the floor cannot be hit by binding alone (§6). The fix proposed in criterion 9 is a test-only `deck_floor` override; the alternative is a 7-page starting deck, which this note recommends against because turn 1 then shows most of the deck.
- **Water-stain bind face.** E1 requires every page to have a usable Bind face, so the foul page can be bound as a way to shelve it (§5 page 21). If the Board prefers an unbindable foul page, E1 needs the words "every page offered as a reward" instead of "every page in the build".
